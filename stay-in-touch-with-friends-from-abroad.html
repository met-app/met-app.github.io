How to stay in touch with friends you made abroad

Updated November 3, 2026

An exchange semester, a working holiday or a year as an expat leaves you with friends from everywhere. Then everyone goes home, and the group chat slowly goes quiet. Staying close across distance is possible, but it takes a few deliberate habits.

Before you leave: save the details

The best time to collect everyone's details is before the goodbye party. Note where each person is going home to, how you met and how they prefer to be reached. A shared QR code makes this quick: in MET, each person shows their profile code and the others scan it.

Know where everyone ends up

After a year abroad, friends scatter across a dozen countries. A map of where they live turns a vague "we should meet up" into a concrete plan, because you can see who's near you and who's near where you're traveling next.

Reach out on occasions

  • Birthdays: save them, and send a message every year.
  • Travel: tell friends when you'll be near their city.
  • Anniversaries of when you met or lived together.

Keep the story alive

Write down where and how you met, and one memory you share. Bringing up a shared story is one of the easiest ways to restart a conversation after months of silence.

Visit

Nothing keeps a long-distance friendship alive like seeing each other. When you plan a holiday, check your map first. You might have a friend, and a free guide, in the city you're about to book.
