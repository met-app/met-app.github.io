How to remember the people you meet while traveling

Updated November 3, 2026

Travel is full of people you swear you'll never forget: the dorm mate who shared their snacks on a night bus, the couple you hiked with for three days, the local who showed you the best street food in town. A year later, you remember the moment but not the name, or you have their Instagram but no idea where they actually live.

Here's a simple system that works, whether you're on a two-week holiday or a year of backpacking.

1. Write it down the same day

Memory fades fastest in the first 24 hours. The best time to save someone is the evening you meet them, while you still remember the details. It takes less than a minute, and it's the habit that makes everything else work.

2. Save more than a name

A name alone is rarely enough to jog your memory later. Save the details that bring the moment back:

  • Where you met: the hostel, the trek, the festival, the city.
  • When: the month and year are usually enough.
  • Where they live: the place they go home to, not just where you met them. That's what tells you who to visit on your next trip.
  • One thing that stood out: what they do, a story they told, a plan you made together.
  • A photo, ideally one of the two of you.

3. Swap contacts in a way you'll actually use

Most travel friendships end with a follow on Instagram and nothing else. Save the contact you'd actually use to say "I'm in your city next week", whether that's WhatsApp, a phone number or an email, alongside their social profiles.

4. Put them on a map

The most useful way to organize travel friends is by where they live. When you plan your next trip, you don't think "who did I meet in 2023?", you think "who do I know in Mexico City?". A map answers that instantly.

This is exactly what MET is built for. You pin each friend in the city they live in, with their photo, where you met and how to reach them. Countries where you have friends light up, and a search finds anyone by name, city or how you met.

5. Make swapping details effortless

The biggest reason people don't save new friends is friction. In MET, you can show your profile as a QR code. Your new friend scans it and gets your name, city and contacts in one go, and you can scan theirs the same way.

6. Look back before every trip

Before you travel, open your map and check who lives where you're going. A message like "I'm in Lisbon next month, want to grab a coffee?" is how a hostel friendship turns into a lifelong one.
